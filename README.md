## Problem Statement - Addressing the Digital Divide to Uphold Digital Rights as Human Rights
The digital divide represents a significant barrier to equality, as millions of people globally lack access to essential digital technologies like smartphones, computers, and the internet. This unequal access exacerbates social and economic disparities, particularly affecting marginalized groups such as those in poverty, rural communities, elderly individuals, and people experiencing homelessness.

Without reliable access to digital tools and the internet, these populations face limitations in their ability to communicate, access critical information, find employment, pursue education, and participate in civic life.

Moreover, even those with access to technology may lack the digital literacy skills necessary to fully engage with these resources, further widening the gap between users. The divide also extends to the production of digital content, with educational and societal disparities creating an imbalance between those who consume digital media and those empowered to create and share it.

Addressing this issue requires implementing policies and initiatives that promote:
- Equitable access to digital technology for all individuals
- Enhanced digital literacy and skills training
- Strong protections for online privacy and data security
- Freedom of expression and inclusion in the digital world

This digital divide threatens to undermine human rights in the Information Age. It is imperative to develop solutions that ensure equal access to digital technologies, empowering all individuals to exercise their digital rights and participate fully in modern society.

## Accessibility Solution at Scale in STEM as a Discipline
**Context**: STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) disciplines play a critical role in the systematic creation and application of knowledge to solve real-world problems. As an individual with roots in Biafra, I have observed that infrastructural solutions must be culturally and contextually tailored to the specific needs of local communities. In regions like South East Africa, where the climate is hot and environmental conditions differ significantly from those in temperate zones like the United Kingdom, the design and implementation of infrastructure must reflect local realities.

**Problem Definition**: The challenge lies in the current application of STEM principles, which often fails to account for cultural and environmental variations in different regions. Many infrastructural designs are developed with a one-size-fits-all mindset, ignoring the specific needs of diverse populations. In regions such as Biafra, applying STEM as a discipline requires not only the technical and scientific rigour of problem-solving but also a systematic integration of cultural and environmental considerations to ensure relevance, efficiency, and sustainability.

**Objective**: The objective is to develop a methodology within the STEM discipline that systematically incorporates local environmental factors, cultural values, and societal needs into the design process. This methodology will not only address the functional aspects of infrastructure but also align with the cultural and artistic expressions of the community, resulting in solutions that are both effective and culturally appropriate.
